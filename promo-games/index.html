From your customer's side

A promo game gives people something to do while they wait, and a reason to have another go. Here is how it goes from their side of the counter.

01

They scan

A QR code on the table, the till or the menu. The game opens in their phone's browser: nothing to download, no sign-up.

02

They see what's on offer

Your prize is on the first screen, in your words: “Top score this week: coffee and cake on us.”

03

They play while they wait

About half a minute a go, with one thumb. Your name, your colours and your product are on screen the whole time.

04

A reason to try again

The board shows how close they got: “12 more to pass Big Dave.” Beating a friend, or the name at the top, takes another go.

You choose the prize and you run it. We can't tell you what it does for your takings: we don't have those numbers yet, and we won't make them up. Play the demo and judge the pull for yourself.

How it works

01

A 20-minute chat

You tell us about your business or event and who you want to reach. We suggest a simple game idea that fits, and you say yes or no.

02

About two weeks to build

Your logo, colours and products, in a one-tap game anyone can pick up in seconds. You play it before it goes live and ask for changes.

03

Three months live

You get a link and QR code artwork for print, and a board that gives players something to beat. Then it switches off, unless you want to keep it running.

Good for

  • A café or shop: “top score this week wins a free coffee”, with a QR code at the till.
  • A restaurant or takeaway: something to play between ordering and eating, with the code on the menu or the table.
  • A pub or bar: a weekly top score for the regulars, played in the queue at the bar.
  • Race weeks and festivals: something for visitors to play in the queue or at your stand.
  • A charity fundraiser: a leaderboard people share with friends.
  • A launch or anniversary: something people actually play, rather than another leaflet.

Questions

Who am I dealing with?

Keith, who runs ai.doo on the Isle of Man and builds the games. Email or WhatsApp, and it's him who answers.

How do I pay, and when?

Nothing until you've said yes to a game idea. Then a £300 deposit by bank transfer starts the build, and the rest is due when your game goes live.

The deposit pays for the build, so it isn't refunded once work starts. If you play your game before launch and decide not to go live, you owe nothing more.

How does a winner get the prize?

They show the winner's screen on their phone at the counter. It has a moving clock, so it can't be a screenshot, and your staff tap it once when the prize is handed over.

The demo has an example: finish a game and tap “See the winner's screen”. In your game, only the real winner would get it.

How will I know anyone played?

We'll send you the number of plays each week. Players stay anonymous: a nickname at most.

What makes it more than £750?

£750 is one of our simple one-tap games, themed to you, for one location. A brand-new game idea, separate boards for several locations or a longer run costs more. You get the price in writing before you pay anything.

What happens after three months?

It switches off. If you'd like to keep it, say so before then and we'll agree a monthly price.
